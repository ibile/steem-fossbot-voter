How this is worked out

An estimate only. PAYE is worked out cumulatively across the tax year, so HMRC’s figure can differ by a pound or two — and anything your employer adds mid-month (bank holiday cover, sleep-ins, a rate change) won’t be here unless you enter it.

Shift types

£/hr or × basic — tap the button beside an amount to switch. A multiplier is the total rate for those hours, so double pay is ×2, and it follows your basic rate when that changes. Top-up only is for hours that are already inside another entry and just earn an enhancement — bank holiday hours inside a night shift, say. They add the extra per hour and nothing else, so the hours are never counted twice. Starts is only used for calendar events — leave it blank for an all-day entry. Uplift is the extra per hour on top of basic — your payslip shows these as their own line. Own rate replaces the basic rate entirely. Unpaid just marks the day.
