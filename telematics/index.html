Score your driving the way an insurer would

Your phone already has the sensors a telematics box uses. This reads them live — GPS for speed and position, the accelerometer and gyroscope for forces — and scores speeding, braking, acceleration and cornering on every trip. Nothing is uploaded; the data stays on this device.

Live tracking will not work in this view. This page is embedded inside another app, which is not letting it read your location, so a trip would record nothing at all. Open it from its own web address to track a real drive. The demo works here either way.

How it scores

Events are counted per 100 km and weighted by severity, so a long motorway run is not flattered and a short trip to the shops is not punished. Each measure decays smoothly — one bad moment costs you, it does not wipe you out.

What this is not. A browser tab cannot track you in the background — the screen has to stay on and this page has to stay open, so it will not silently log every journey the way an insurer's app does. It also has no map-matched speed limit database, so you set the limit yourself as you drive. Treat the score as a mirror of your own driving, not as anything an insurer would accept.

How the numbers are made

Speed comes from a constant-acceleration Kalman filter over GPS, which also yields longitudinal g without differentiating noisy samples. Gravity is low-passed out of the accelerometer only while the reading is plausibly static, giving a stable vertical axis. Yaw rate is the gyroscope component along that axis, so lateral g — speed multiplied by yaw rate — is correct no matter how the phone sits in the cradle. The forward axis is solved by correlating horizontal acceleration against the GPS estimate during straight-line speed changes, then high-frequency detail is blended onto the GPS trend. With no gyroscope it falls back to GPS heading changes; with no motion sensor at all it runs on GPS alone.
